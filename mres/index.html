The term "multi-religion election"—representing the practical application of the multi-religion election system—refers to a reform of church elections. Church elections are a practice unique to the Nordic countries, where they have introduced democracy into the Protestant denominations; other religious communities based in the Nordic countries do not hold corresponding religious elections. Sweden has freedom of religion, meaning that everyone has the right to decide for themselves which religion to belong to—or whether to belong to none at all—but it is democracy that has granted freedom of religion to all religious communities. In a democratic country, this entails, among other things, that a refugee may be granted protection in Sweden due to persecution based on their religion. A defining characteristic of a secular state is the complete separation of state and church, as well as of religion and politics. The notion—held by many—that Sweden is a Christian country and must therefore have a religious party in the Riksdag conflicts with the fundamental principle of separation mentioned above. The same applies to the idea that Sweden, as an EU member, must have a religious party in the European Parliament; this, too, runs counter to the fundamental principle of separation. Regardless of whether the "Multi-religion Election" holds official political or legal significance in Sweden, the aim is to ensure that the 78-year-old conflict between Israel and Palestine is not left for the next generation to resolve—as politicians of previous generations have done. More information about the Multi-Religion Election System is available at MRES (https://mres.multireligionvalsystem.eu.org/) , more information about Multi-Religion Elections is available at MRE (https://www.multireligionvalsystem.eu.org/mre), and information on Multi-Religion Support is available at https://multi-religion-support.multireligionvalsystem.eu.org/altirenativ-till-SST-stiftelse. More information on what constitutes a complete separation between state and church, as well as between religion and politics, can be found at https://multireligionvalsystem.eu.org/ofullbordade-separationen-stat-kyrka-religion-politik.html
