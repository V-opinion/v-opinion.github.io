Multireligionvalsystem (MRES)

A democratic and interfaith governance framework proposed as a reform of Nordic church elections and a conflict-resolution model for multireligious societies.

Core Definition: The Multi-Religion Election System (MRES) extends democratic election principles across religious communities while maintaining secular state neutrality and a clear separation between religion and politics.

Read the analysis on completing the separation of church, state, religion, and politics

Core Components of the MRES Framework

Starting from the Nordic experience of democratic elections within Protestant churches, MRES outlines three institutional mechanisms for inclusive, non-partisan religious governance:

Multi-Religion Election (MRE)

The practical electoral mechanism extending democratic voting models beyond existing church elections to broader, voluntary religious participation.

Explore Multi-Religion Elections (MRE)

Faith Representatives Chamber (FRC)

A proposed representative body independent of political parties and state parliaments, designed to support interfaith dialogue, civic accountability, and mediation.

Learn about the Faith Representatives Chamber

Multi-Religion Support

A proposed financial framework designed to replace state-linked religious subsidies and party-affiliated church funding with transparent, non-tax revenue models.

Read about Multi-Religion Support

Objectives & Theoretical Foundations

MRES engages with research traditions in consociationalism, electoral system design, religion and politics, and peacebuilding. Its primary institutional goals are to:

  • Mitigate religiously framed political conflicts through structured interfaith representation.
  • Protect religious minorities and non-believers under secular democratic law.
  • Prevent structural or state-backed domination by any single religious tradition.
  • Strengthen democratic legitimacy, youth participation, and gender equality within faith communities.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Multi-Religion Election System (MRES)?

The Multi-Religion Election System (MRES) is a democratic and interfaith governance framework proposed as a reform of Nordic-style church elections and a conflict-resolution model for multireligious societies. It connects religious freedom, internal community democracy, and secular state neutrality.

How does MRES differ from the Swedish church election (kyrkovalet)?

Traditional church elections in the Nordic context apply to specific church structures—such as the Church of Sweden (Svenska kyrkan)—and often involve political party nominations. MRES examines reforming that model to address broader multi-faith representation, transparent governance, and clear boundaries between religious institutions and state politics.

Why connect religious governance to peacebuilding?

Proponents of MRES argue that unresolved grievances tied to religious identity, unequal recognition, or politicized religion can deepen social division and protracted conflicts. Structured interfaith representation and democratic norms are proposed as tools to encourage dialogue, minority protection, and peaceful conflict resolution.

Sammanfattning på svenska: Vad är ett multireligionvalsystem (MRES)?

Ett multireligionvalsystem (MRES) är ett förslag till demokratisk reform av kyrkovalet och en modell för dialog och konflikthantering i mångreligiösa samhällen. Modellen utgår från nordiska erfarenheter av demokratiska val i trossamfund, men diskuterar hur demokratiska principer, religionsfrihet samt en tydlig åtskillnad mellan stat, politik och religion kan utformas bredare.
